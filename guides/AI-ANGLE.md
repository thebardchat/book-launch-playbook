# How to Talk About AI Co-Writing

The hardest marketing problem for AI-assisted creators: how do you talk about the collaboration without apologizing for it?

## The Framework

**Don't say:** "AI wrote my book."
**Don't say:** "I used AI to help with some editing."
**Say:** "I wrote my book with AI."

The word "with" does all the work. It implies partnership. It implies both sides contributed. It implies neither side could have done it alone.

## The Metaphors That Work

Pick one and stick with it across your campaign:

- **"Like a potter doesn't invent the clay."** You brought the raw material. AI found the form.
- **"I brought the life. AI brought the craft."** Clear, simple, undeniable.
- **"Two things meeting in the middle."** Neither is the author alone. Both are the author together.
- **"I recorded. AI shaped."** For process-focused audiences.

## The Questions You'll Get (and How to Answer Them)

### "So AI wrote your book?"
> No. I wrote my book with AI. I recorded raw voice — observations, feelings, stories from my real life. AI shaped those into prose. The voice is mine. The shaping is the collaboration.

### "Isn't that cheating?"
> Is using a word processor cheating? Is hiring an editor cheating? Every author uses tools. This is a tool that can understand what I'm trying to say and help me say it better. The ideas, the observations, the emotions — those are mine. The craft of turning them into readable prose — that's the collaboration.

### "Could you have done it without AI?"
> No. And AI couldn't have done it without me. That's the whole point.

### "Are you worried about AI replacing writers?"
> I'm a concrete dispatch operator. I was never going to write a book the traditional way. AI didn't replace a writer — it created one. The question isn't whether AI replaces writers. The question is how many people have stories they'll never tell because they don't have access to the craft. Now they do.

### "How much did AI actually write?"
> Every word started as my voice — recorded while driving, walking, living. AI shaped those words into prose. It's like asking how much of a building the architect designed vs. the builder built. Both. All of it. Together.

## The Angles by Platform

### LinkedIn / Professional
Lead with the tool angle. "I used Claude by Anthropic as a co-writing partner." Name the tool. The tech community respects specificity.

### Facebook / Personal
Lead with the personal angle. "I recorded voice memos on my drive home and an AI helped me turn them into a book." The human story first.

### Reddit / Tech
Lead with the process. "Here's the actual pipeline: voice memo → transcript → AI shaping → editing → publish. AMA." They want the details.

### Twitter/X
Lead with the take. "People ask if AI wrote my book. No. I wrote my book with AI. There's a difference and the difference matters." The hot take gets engagement.

## The One Rule

**Never apologize for using AI.**

The moment you apologize, you've told the audience that AI is something to be ashamed of. It's not. You found a tool that let you tell your story. The tool doesn't diminish the story. The story is why the tool matters.
